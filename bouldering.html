ON THE WALL

Bouldering training

Twelve weeks. Three movable sessions. Better movement, stronger climbing, and time to recover.

V5 project level · Indoor bouldering · Updated 31 October 2026

The three sessions

  • A: Limit and projects. Your hardest session: near-limit attempts on V4-V5 problems while fresh. Friday by default.
  • B: Volume and technique. Lots of easy-to-moderate problems (mostly V0-V3, RPE 4-6) with one skill focus per block. Most of your mileage lives here.
  • C: Slopers and overhangs. Position and tension drills for your two weak styles at moderate effort (RPE 5-7). Not a second limit session.
  • Counting: every try in its counted block counts, successful or not, even a fall on move one. In A count hard tries only; its easy warm-up and rehearsal are separate. In B count all climbing tries, including the 6-8 easy warm-up tries, in every week. In C count every drill try; its easy warm-up is separate. Use approximate totals and a time box. Simplify after two unsuccessful tries. Stop at the time limit or try ceiling, whichever comes first.
  • Session length: standard visits last about 100-120 minutes. Optional longer visits last about 135-170 minutes and redistribute some weekly easy mileage. Four hours is available time, not a required training dose.

Phases

  • Weeks 1-3, Foundation: repeatable footwork, sloper positions, controlled steep climbing, and a workload you recover from.
  • Week 4, Check: repeat the benchmarks with about 40% less volume.
  • Weeks 5-7, Build: link hard moves, use hooks and hip turns on steeper terrain.
  • Week 8, Deload and check: about half of week 7, benchmarks at RPE 8 or lower.
  • Weeks 9-11, Transfer: apply the movement to V4-V5 projects and unfamiliar problems.
  • Week 12, Taper and check: repeat the benchmarks fresh, then choose your next block.

Week by week

Counts are ceilings, not quotas. A counts hard attempts; B counts all easy/moderate tries including its climbing warm-up; C counts drill tries after its warm-up. A and C warm-ups stay easy and are separate. In B/C use the time limit OR the try ceiling, whichever comes first. Every try counts, successful or not. After two unsuccessful tries, simplify or choose another problem. B includes its 6-8 easy climbing warm-up tries throughout all 12 weeks: week 1 therefore leaves about 10-12 technique tries, and the peak about 20-22. This is an intentional gentle ramp; do not start excluding the warm-up later.

Week 1 will feel light. With all six A test tries used, about two project tries remain. After B's 6-8 warm-up tries and four test tries, about 6-8 technique tries remain. After C's four test tries, about six drill tries remain. This is intentional: establish a baseline and check recovery before adding work.

Increase only when the preceding workload is well tolerated during climbing and the next morning. Repeat a week when needed. If your recent workload was lower, begin below these numbers; if you have been climbing fewer than three times weekly, begin with two climbing visits and one recovery visit for the first two weeks. Do not jump directly to the peak ranges.

Scheduling with Friday as the anchor

  • A and C each require at least one climbing-free day beforehand. Do not schedule C the day after B, even short easy B. Allow at least 48 hours between A and C, and between any two demanding finger sessions, measured between session start times. Use more rest when soreness remains. C stays moderate; if it becomes RPE 8 or higher, shorten it and count those hard attempts against A's weekly ceiling.
  • Friday is A by default. If you climbed Thursday, Friday is B or recovery, and A moves to another rested day. Do not force a Friday hard session.
  • B may follow A or C on the next day only when there is no focal pain, movement is normal and skin tolerates it. That back-to-back B is 45-60 minutes of easy climbing INCLUDING rests and its first 6-8 easy warm-up tries, RPE 4-5, with a ceiling of 12-16 short tries or the weekly B allowance, whichever is lower. Use the lower end initially. This is climbing-block time; add about 15 minutes of off-wall preparation and a short cooldown, for a visit of roughly 65-85 minutes. Stop earlier if skin or movement deteriorates. It REPLACES full B; do not add the missed mileage elsewhere. If recovery is poor, rest instead.
  • No three consecutive climbing days in this block. The only reduced climbing module is the short easy B defined above; a recovery visit means no climbing. Use at most three climbing sessions in any rolling seven days: one A, one C and one B. This is a maximum, not a requirement to complete all three. Missed sessions are skipped, not made up. Check the previous and following week when days move.

Example schedules

  • Monday + Wednesday: Monday C, Wednesday B, Friday A.
  • Tuesday + Thursday: Tuesday A, Thursday C, Friday short easy B if recovered.
  • Wednesday + Saturday: Wednesday C, Friday A, Saturday short easy B if recovered.
  • Saturday + Sunday: Friday A, Saturday rest, Sunday C only if Sunday's start is at least 48 hours after Friday's and recovery is normal. Otherwise Sunday is short easy B or rest. Skip the unused module; do not force all three sessions into this weekend.

Recovery visit: 15-25 minutes of easy walking/cycling, floor core and gentle mobility if symptom-free. No climbing, hangs or hard gripping.

Intensity guide

Use movement effort (RPE, 1-10) rather than treating gym grades as exact.

  • RPE 3-4: very easy, no pump. Typically V0-V1.
  • RPE 5-6: comfortable to moderately challenging, repeatable. Typically V1-V3.
  • RPE 7: challenging, but you keep deliberate feet and can repeat after resting. Often V2-V4, lower on weak styles.
  • RPE 8-9: near-limit. Some failed attempts, but no desperate catching or painful gripping. Usually V4-V5 or a short section.
  • RPE 10: all-out. Not prescribed.

Grades are only starting points: a V2 sloper can load you harder than a V4 jug problem. Judge by effort, and count a nominally easy move as hard if it really reaches RPE 8-9.

Warm-up (A and C: 30 minutes, B: 15 minutes)

  1. 5 minutes of easy walking, cycling or stairs (RPE 2-3).
  2. One round of joint prep: 8 wrist circles each way, 10 gentle hand opens and closes, 8 shoulder circles each way, 6 hip rotations and 8 ankle rocks per side.
  3. Shoulders: 1 × 10 wall push-up-plus and 1 × 10 light band external rotations per side.
  4. A and C only: 6-8 short easy problems over about 15 minutes, V0-V1 rising to V2 (V3 only if it feels easy), RPE 5 at most, 60-90 seconds rest.

On B days, steps 1-3 are enough: your first 6-8 problems at V0-V1 are the rest of the warm-up. No hangboard warm-up, maximal squeezes or bouncing onto holds.

Session A: Limit and projects

Purpose: learn hard moves and link them while fresh.

  • Rehearsal (10 min): 2 problems repeated twice (4 short attempts total) at V2-V3, RPE 5-6, chosen to match the style of the day's projects.
  • Project block (up to 55 min): 2-3 problems at about V4-V5, up to 4 hard attempts on any one problem, within the weekly total of 8-12 (6 in benchmark weeks). Rest 3-5 minutes between hard attempts, the full 5 after a near-limit go. The block ends at 55 minutes OR the weekly try ceiling, whichever comes first. Twelve is a ceiling, not a quota: with longer rests you may manage only about 8-10 quality tries. Never shorten rest to fit more tries; unused tries are not made up.
  • Weeks 1-3: RPE 8 at most. Build and Transfer weeks can reach RPE 9 if you stay controlled and pain-free.
  • Linking: inside the same hard-attempt cap, replace full starts with 2-4-move sections when useful.
  • Weakness link: in Build and Transfer weeks, make at least one project sloper-based or on 15-30° overhanging terrain with usable holds.
  • One change per attempt: name it first (for example, hip in before reaching). After two identical failures, change your beta, shorten to a section, or move on.
  • Skip: campus boarding, maximal dynos to small holds, monos, small pockets and forced full crimps.

2 hours: warm-up 30 / rehearsal 10 / projects 55 / accessories 15 / cooldown 10.

Longer visit option: after at least two well-recovered weeks at the current workload, optionally use a 15-20 minute food/rest break and 20-30 minutes of very easy large-hold movement (RPE 3-4). This replaces some of that week's B mileage; log these tries and subtract them from B's allowance. No additional hard attempts. Do this before accessories and the normal 10-minute cooldown. Total visit is about 155-170 minutes, not a required four hours. Stop when quality declines; remaining social or observation time is optional.

Session B: Volume and technique

Purpose: build movement vocabulary and efficient feet, balance and route reading through lots of successful climbing.

After the first 6-8 very easy warm-up tries, divide the remaining allowance approximately into 20% quiet feet, 20% hips and balance, 30% read and refine, and 30% mixed styles. Repeats count as tries. Include comfortable slopers or juggy mild overhangs in the mixed block, below project intensity. Use 60-75 minutes INCLUDING rests for all B climbing, rather than chasing a quota.

  1. Quiet and precise feet (V0-V2, RPE 4-5): look at each foothold until your foot is placed. Aim for no scraping and no repositioning.
  2. Hips and balance (V1-V3, RPE 4-5): climb the same problem square-on, then with one hip turned in. Pause 1 second in a stable position before each reach.
  3. Read, climb, refine (V1-V3, RPE 5-6): preview hands, feet and the likely crux. After the first go, change one inefficiency and repeat.
  4. Mixed style (V1-V3, RPE 5-6): slab, vertical, slopers, mild overhang, different hold types. Choose new problems over repeats.

Rules: forearm pump 3/10 or less, about 1-2 minutes rest between problems, at least 60% of problems on large holds. End a block when your feet get noisy. Check your skin halfway through. No timed circuits, 4×4s or finishers.

Standard B visit: off-wall warm-up 15 minutes; climbing including easy climbing warm-up and rests 60-75; accessories 15; cooldown 10. Total 100-115 minutes. Stop at the time or count ceiling even if you have more time available.

Longer B visit: only after two well-recovered weeks, a 15-20 minute break and optional 20-30 minute easy technique block may spread the SAME weekly B allowance over more time. Keep that extra block at RPE 3-4 on generous holds; do not add 50% more problems. Accessories and the 10-minute cooldown come after the final climbing block. Total about 135-165 minutes. A four-hour stay is optional, not a training requirement.

Session C: Slopers and overhangs

Purpose: improve position and tension without turning this into a second limit session.

Terrain: big, comfortable slopers on vertical to gently overhanging walls, and juggy overhangs 15-30° past vertical. If that's too hard, start shallower. A steeper angle is earned by control, not by the calendar.

Drills. Use a total of 10-16 short drill tries in normal weeks, or 8 in reduced weeks, within 45-55 minutes INCLUDING rests. Each try starts when you leave the ground to attempt a short sequence. Every try counts, whether you finish, repeat the sequence or fall on move one. Select 3-4 drills, roughly 2-4 tries each, stopping at the weekly ceiling. Always include sloper work, steep tension and a hook drill. Alternate heel and toe hooks across lower-volume weeks. If you attempt a sequence twice without success, simplify it; do not hide repeated falls inside one counted problem.

  1. Sloper position mapping (RPE 5-6): 3-5-move low sequences. Stop in two supported positions for 2-3 seconds each.
  2. Sloper linking (RPE 6-7): 4-6-move problems. On the second go, change one foot or hip position.
  3. Feet-on steep climbing (RPE 6-7): 4-6 moves on generous holds, feet intentionally connected.
  4. Square versus hip turn (RPE 5-6): the same 3-5 steep moves both ways, then keep the more efficient version.
  5. Heel hooks (RPE 5-6): low sequences, left and right, with 2-3 gentle placements. Load gradually and release.
  6. Toe hooks (RPE 5-6): the same idea. Lift with the top of the shoe and use the other foot for opposition.

Rest 2-3 minutes between C tries; use a longer rest if needed to regain deliberate feet. No continuous circuit or training to failure. If a sequence climbs above RPE 7, lower the angle or use larger feet. If it stays hard, count those attempts toward that week's A hard-attempt cap and trim A.

Sloper cues

  • Brush and dry the hold. Use as much comfortable palm and finger contact as it allows.
  • Move your hips until the pull matches the useful face of the hold. Lower under the hand often helps a top sloper, and side-facing slopers may need lateral opposition.
  • Put weight through your feet before moving a hand.
  • Keep your wrists in a comfortable range.
  • "Hips in" is an option, not a rule. Test a small position change before squeezing harder.

Overhang and hook cues

  • Keep ribs and pelvis connected with light abdominal and glute tension.
  • Press or pull through the toes so your legs don't trail.
  • Set the foot or hook, move your hips, then reach.
  • Let the shoulder blade move.
  • Heel hook: pull gradually with hamstring and hip, and don't wrench the knee.
  • Practise hooks low enough to step off, and avoid positions where a trapped foot could stay hooked in a fall.
  • Optional board work: swap one drill for a large-hold sequence on a shallow board setting at RPE 5-7. Record the angle and holds.
  • Standard C visit: warm-up 30 minutes; drills including rests 45-55; accessories 20; cooldown 10; log 5. Total 110-120 minutes. Stop earlier when useful work is finished.
  • Longer C visit: after two well-recovered weeks, optionally use a 15-20 minute break and 20-30 minutes of very easy large-hold technique (RPE 3-4). These tries replace some B mileage that week and are logged against B's allowance. No additional RPE 6-7 weakness block. Do not force V2-V4 problems to feel easy. Finish accessories and the normal cooldown after climbing. Total about 145-170 minutes; there is no requirement to train for four hours.

Accessory work

Do these after climbing, never before hard attempts. Leave at least 3 reps in reserve and keep your grip light so they don't become finger training. Rest 30-90 seconds.

Package A (after A)

  • push-up-plus 2 × 8-12.
  • band or side-lying light-dumbbell external rotation 2 × 12-15 per side.
  • supported wrist extension with a light dumbbell 2 × 12-15 per side, lowering slowly.

Package B (after B)

  • light chest-supported or one-arm dumbbell row 2 × 8-12 per side.
  • dead bug 2 × 6 per side, exhaling for 3 seconds per rep.

Package C (after C)

  • wall slide 2 × 8-12.
  • side plank 2 × 20-30 seconds per side.
  • bent-knee hollow hold 2 × 15-25 seconds.

Skip or substitute anything painful. Weeks 4, 8 and 12 use one set per exercise. When both sets reach the top of the range easily in two sessions, add the smallest load increment or the repetitions, never both. No extra heavy pull-ups, finger curls or grip work in this block. If you lift heavy elsewhere, remove overlapping pulling work instead of stacking it.

Cooldown (every session)

Walk easily for 5 minutes (RPE 1-2). Then one round: 90/90 hip switches × 6 per side, ankle rocks × 8 per side, thoracic rotations × 6 per side, and a gentle forearm stretch for 20 seconds per side. Stay at a mild 2-3/10 stretch with no joint or tendon pain. On longer visits the same 10-minute cooldown is sufficient. A cooldown eases you out of the session. It isn't protection against too much load.

Hangboarding and heavy finger loading

No hangboard or campus work in these 12 weeks. Climbing already loads your fingers a lot, and tendons adapt more slowly than muscle. One randomized trial found that 10 weeks of hangboarding added to regular climbing raised finger strength in intermediate and advanced climbers, but it says nothing about someone with 3 months of experience. Reassess after week 12 if you are recovered, pain-free and still limited by grip after technique work. Any future finger work should replace some climbing load, not add to it.

Progression and plateaus

Progress only when all of these are true:

  • No finger, elbow or shoulder pain during the session, afterwards or the next morning.
  • Normal movement and no lingering focal tenderness before the next session.
  • Good feet and shoulder control last through the final planned problems.
  • Sleep, recovery and motivation feel normal for you.

Then pick one:

  • More volume at the same difficulty.
  • The same volume with one slightly harder problem, a longer link or a small angle increase.
  • The same difficulty and volume with cleaner execution: fewer foot cuts and hand adjustments, lower RPE.

Don't make holds smaller and terrain steeper at the same time. A week or two without a new grade is normal. After three well-recovered weeks with no progress in moves, links or technique:

  1. Film yourself or ask a stronger climber to spot one limiting movement.
  2. Change one project style or one C drill, keeping volume unchanged.
  3. If performance is also dropping or soreness is building, take an early deload: about half the volume for a week.

Don't answer a plateau with another hard day or a hangboard session.

Fatigue and pain rules

  • Mild, diffuse muscle soreness, normal movement: stay in the lower RPE range, cut volume by about 25% and don't progress that week.
  • Soreness that changes your movement or lasts beyond about 72 hours: rest or take a non-climbing recovery visit. On return, cut volume by about 50% and repeat the week.
  • Very poor sleep, unusual fatigue, or warm-up problems that feel about 2 RPE harder than normal: no A. If the warm-up normalizes and nothing hurts, do a half-volume B at RPE 5 or lower. Otherwise stop.
  • New focal finger or pulley, elbow or shoulder pain, even mild: stop climbing for the day. Don't keep testing it. Get it assessed if it lasts into the next day, recurs or limits normal use.
  • Pop or snap with pain, swelling, sudden weakness, numbness or inability to grip normally: stop and get prompt medical assessment.
  • Skin split, bleeding or raw enough to change your grip: end climbing.

End the climbing portion when:

  • Two consecutive tries lose control despite full rest.
  • Pump stays above 3/10 after 5-10 minutes of rest.
  • You catch slips with uncontrolled arms or compensate with forced crimps.
  • You reach the time limit or relevant try ceiling.
  • You cannot make a safe landing decision.

Take at most one 10-minute off-wall reset. If quality does not return, stop. Do not fill spare time with hangboarding or heavy pulling.

Aim for 8-9 hours of sleep and regular meals with enough food to recover. Keep rest-day cardio easy. No weight-loss phase is needed for this plan.

Benchmarks (baseline in week 1, repeat in weeks 4, 8 and 12)

Each test sits inside the matching session, right after the warm-up. The four B test tries and four C test tries count toward their matching weekly ceilings. All tests replace ordinary work. Do the remaining session at that week's reduced allowance; no longer-visit extensions in weeks 4, 8 or 12. Take 48-72 hours off climbing before the A test, stay at RPE 8 or lower, and match holds, angle, warm-up and rest times as closely as you can.

  1. Controlled performance (in A): two repeatable large-hold reference problems at V3-V4, one in a familiar style and one moderately steep. Up to 3 tries each with 3-5 minutes rest. Record sends out of 2, attempts to send, best controlled moves and RPE. This reserves 6 of the week's A attempts even if some test moves feel easier. In weeks 4, 8 and 12 it replaces all normal A projecting.
  2. Footwork and efficiency (in B): two easy V1-V2 problems, 2 tries each, RPE 4-6. Record noisy placements, foot readjustments and unnecessary hand adjustments.
  3. Weak-style execution (in C): one sloper sequence and one juggy 15-30° overhang sequence, 2 tries each, RPE 5-7. Record controlled moves, unplanned foot cuts, hook placement and release, and RPE.

Use a persistent board or spray-wall sequence if your gym has one. If the gym resets a reference, set a new baseline instead of comparing unlike problems. Once a reference gets easy, keep its result and add a harder one for the next block.

Success at 12 weeks means more repeatable V4-V5 movement, better control on slopers and overhangs, and a workload you recover from. A V6 may happen, but it's not a required outcome and not a reason to force tiny holds or add load.

Evidence notes

  • A randomized trial in student climbers found fewer shoulder injuries with a closed-chain, eccentric strength program than without one. The shoulder work here is a practical selection in that spirit, not that exact program (Kozin et al., Acta of Bioengineering and Biomechanics, 2021; doi 10.37190/ABB-01828-2021-01).
  • A randomized trial found that 10 weeks of hangboarding on top of regular climbing increased finger strength in intermediate and advanced climbers (Hermans et al., Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, 2022; doi 10.3389/fspor.2022.888158).
  • The attempt caps, spacing and progression rules are conservative coaching judgment. They haven't been validated as a protocol for climbers with 3 months of experience, and no plan removes injury risk or guarantees a grade.

## Week by week

A = controlled limit/project bouldering, normally Friday. B = volume and technique. C = slopers and overhangs. Counts below are ceilings for attempts AFTER the shared warm-up, not targets you must finish.

For A, the number is hard attempts only; add the four easy rehearsal attempts described in A. For B and C, the number includes EVERY drill attempt, repeat and partial sequence. One attempt begins when you leave the ground, even if you fall on move one. Do not add free climbing afterwards.

| Week | A hard attempts | B drill attempts | C drill attempts | Focus and change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 8 maximum, including baseline | 12 | 8 | Establish baseline within these budgets. A at RPE 8 maximum; record recovery the following morning. |
| 2 | 10 | 14 | 10 | Same difficulty as week 1. Add attempts only if week 1 recovery was good. |
| 3 | 10 | 14 | 10 | Hold volume. Improve feet, hip timing and links; do not raise difficulty and volume together. |
| 4 | 6 benchmark attempts | 8 | 6 | Repeat benchmarks. Keep other work easy. One set per accessory exercise. |
| 5 | 10 | 14 | 10 | Return to week 3 volume. A can reach RPE 9 if controlled and pain-free. |
| 6 | 12 | 16 | 12 | Add attempts at the same difficulty if fully recovered. |
| 7 | 12 | 16 | 12 | Hold volume. Link project sections; progress one problem or one technical constraint. |
| 8 | 6 benchmark attempts | 8 | 6 | Deload. Benchmarks stay at RPE 8 or below. No extra project attempts. |
| 9 | 10 | 14 | 10 | Rebuild. Apply learned movement to unfamiliar V3–V5 problems. |
| 10 | 12 | 14 | 12 | Progress either difficulty OR attempts, not both. Keep B and C at their usual RPE. |
| 11 | 12 | 14 | 12 | Keep working the same two or three projects. Prioritize clean links and sends over more attempts. |

If the next row is too large a step, repeat the current week. A calendar week is not permission to increase load.

## Key principles and how to use the modules

This plan uses your current profile: age 19, 78 kg, three months of climbing, V5 project maximum and no current injury. No equipment clarification is needed to start; a light dumbbell or water bottle can replace a band for shoulder work.

The exact attempt limits, spacing and progression rules are conservative coaching prescriptions. They have not been validated as a complete protocol specifically for three-month V5 climbers. No plan eliminates injury risk or guarantees a particular grade after 12 weeks.

### Intensity

Use movement RPE rather than treating gym grades as a precise measure.

| RPE | Meaning here | Typical starting selection with a V5 maximum |
|---|---|---|
| 3–4 | Very easy; plenty of control and no pump | V0–V1 |
| 5–6 | Comfortable to moderately challenging; generally repeatable | V1–V3 |
| 7 | Challenging, but you can keep deliberate feet and repeat after resting | Often V2–V4, lower on weak styles |
| 8–9 | Near-limit movement; some failed attempts, but no desperate catching or painful gripping | Usually V4–V5 or a short section |
| 10 | All-out effort with no reserve | Not prescribed |

Grades are only starting points. A V2 sloper can load you harder than a V4 jug problem. Keep B at RPE 4–6 and C at RPE 5–7, even if that means V0–V2. Count a nominally easy move as hard if its actual effort reaches RPE 8–9.

### Scheduling with Friday fixed

1. Put A on Friday when recovered. Give A and C at least 48 hours between session start times, with a climbing-free day between them; allow 72 hours or more when soreness remains.
2. For this introductory block, use the same 48-hour minimum between all full climbing modules, including B. Check the previous and following week, not just the current calendar week.
3. Assign C to whichever other available day allows that spacing around both Friday and B. Put B on the remaining spaced day. A, B and C do not otherwise need a fixed order.
4. If your available days cannot fit the spacing, convert the conflicting visit to the recovery visit below. Do not squeeze in three climbing days or make up missed attempts.
5. If Friday arrives less than 48 hours after an unplanned climbing session, Friday becomes a recovery visit or easy non-climbing session. Move A to the next properly spaced slot and omit C if needed.
6. Use at most three full climbing sessions, one A and one C in any rolling seven days. Shifting weekdays must not create an extra session across a calendar-week boundary. Keep all RPE 8–9 attempts within that week's A ceiling, including any unexpectedly hard C attempts.

If you have recently been climbing fewer than three times weekly, use two climbing sessions plus one recovery visit for the first two weeks. Start below the listed attempt ceilings if they exceed your recent comfortable workload.

| Other available days | Assignment with Friday A |
|---|---|
| Monday and Wednesday | Monday C, Wednesday B, Friday A |
| Tuesday and Sunday | Tuesday B, Friday A, Sunday C; check Tuesday of the following week |
| Tuesday and Thursday | Tuesday C, Thursday recovery visit, Friday A |
| Wednesday and Saturday | Wednesday C, Friday A, Saturday recovery visit |

Times matter: Friday at 8 pm to Sunday at 10 am is only 38 hours. Shift the Sunday start or make it recovery.

Recovery visit: 15–25 minutes easy walking or cycling at RPE 2–3; video/beta review; the floor core and gentle mobility below if symptom-free. No climbing, hangs, hard gripping or loaded pulling. It can be a short visit; there is no need to fill two hours.

### Shared warm-up for every climbing module — 30 minutes

| Drill | Dose | Rest | Intensity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Walk, bike or easy stairs | 5 minutes | None | RPE 2–3 | Raise temperature |
| Joint movement | 1 round: 8 wrist circles each direction, 10 gentle hand opens/closes, 8 shoulder circles each way, 6 hip rotations each side, 8 ankle rocks each side | Move smoothly between exercises | RPE 1–2; painless range | Prepare comfortable movement ranges |
| Shoulder activation | 1 × 10 wall push-up-plus; 1 × 10 light band external rotations each side | 30 seconds between exercises | RPE 3–4 | Serratus and rotator-cuff activation without fatigue |
| Progressive easy boulders | 6–8 short attempts over about 15 minutes: start V0–V1, progress to V2; one familiar V3 only if easy | 60–90 seconds between attempts | RPE 3 rising to no more than 5 | Gradually load fingers and rehearse feet |

Use easy downclimbs where comfortable. Do not add repeated descents just to create volume. No hangboard warm-up, maximal squeezes or bouncing onto holds.

### Session A — controlled limit and project bouldering

Purpose: learn difficult movement, develop usable strength/power and practice linking moves while fresh.

| Main-work drill | Dose | Rest | Intensity | What it trains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Movement rehearsal | 2 easier problems × 2 attempts = 4 attempts total; 3–6 moves each | 90–120 seconds | RPE 5–6, usually V2–V3 | Rehearse the movement pattern of the day's projects |
| Project attempts | Weekly A cap across 2–3 problems; no more than 4 hard attempts on one problem | 3–5 minutes between hard attempts; take the full 5 after a near-limit go | Weeks 1–3: RPE 8 maximum. Build/transfer: RPE 8–9. Usually V4–V5 | Hard-move execution and high-quality recruitment |
| Linking | Within that SAME attempt cap, replace full starts with 2–4-move sections when useful | 3–5 minutes | Same limit | Learn cruxes without repeatedly exhausting easier moves |

Choose problems with usable holds, predictable feet and safe landings. Include one familiar style and one moderately steep or sloper problem, provided it meets the RPE and control limits. On steeper projects use better holds rather than chasing tiny edges.

Before each attempt: name one change, such as “right hip in before reaching” or “set heel before moving left hand.” Film two attempts if gym rules and others' privacy allow. After two identical failures, change beta, use a shorter section, or move on. Do not keep pulling just to accumulate effort.

Do not add campus boarding, maximal dynos to small holds, monos, small pockets or forced full crimps. Controlled fast movement between generous holds is acceptable when feet and landing remain predictable.

After climbing: accessory package A. Then the shared cooldown and log.

| Version | Time budget, in order |
|---|---|
| 2 hours | Warm-up 30 min → rehearsal 10 → project block 45 → accessories 20 → cooldown 10 → log 5 |
| 4 hours | Warm-up 30 → rehearsal 10 → project block 70 → food/off-wall break 30 → video and beta review 30 → accessories 20 → cooldown 45 → log 5 |

The longer project block spreads the SAME attempts over more rest and analysis. Once your cap or quality limit is reached, climbing is finished.

### Session B — volume and technique

Purpose: accumulate successful movement and learn efficient feet, balance and route reading.

Take the following blocks in order. Each block uses up to four attempts. Week 1's 12 attempts cover the first three blocks. With a cap of 14, add two attempts from block 4; with 16, add four. Use different easy problems for variety.

| Drill | Dose | Rest | Intensity | What it trains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet and precise feet | 2 problems × 2 attempts; look at each foothold until the foot is placed; aim for no scraping or repositioning | 90–150 seconds per attempt | RPE 4–5; V0–V2 | Foot accuracy and reduced unnecessary hand loading |
| Hip-position comparison | 2 problems × 2 attempts; compare square hips with turning one hip toward the wall; pause 1 second in a stable position before reaching | 90–150 seconds | RPE 4–5 | Balance and hip positioning; recognizing when each position helps |
| Read, climb, refine | 2 problems × 2 attempts; first preview hand sequence, feet and likely crux; second attempt changes ONE inefficiency | 2 minutes | RPE 5–6; often V1–V3 | Planning, memory and efficient sequencing |
| Mixed-style practice | Up to 4 single attempts on easy slab, vertical, sloper or mild-overhang problems | 2 minutes | RPE 5–6 | Transfer and movement variety |

Keep forearm pump at 3/10 or less. Choose easier holds or end a block if you start falling because of fatigue. No timed circuits, 4×4s or “finishers” in this block.

In benchmark weeks, perform the four B test attempts first, then fill only the remaining weekly budget with quiet-feet or easy mixed practice.

After climbing: accessory package B. Then cooldown and log.

| Version | Time budget, in order |
|---|---|
| 2 hours | Warm-up 30 min → technique blocks 60 → accessories 15 → cooldown 10 → log 5 |
| 4 hours | Warm-up 30 → technique blocks 80 → food/off-wall break 30 → video, observation and beta review 40 → accessories 15 → cooldown 40 → log 5 |

Keep the SAME attempt cap in both versions. In the longer version, identify foot placements and hip turns in a stronger climber's movement, then review your own footage.

### Session C — slopers and overhangs

Purpose: improve position and tension without making this a second limit session.

Select big, comfortable slopers on vertical to gently overhanging terrain and juggy overhangs around 15–30 degrees past vertical. Start shallower if that is too hard. A steeper angle is earned by control, not by the calendar.

Choose four drills when the cap is 8, five when it is 10, or all six when it is 12. Each chosen drill uses two attempts. Always include at least one sloper drill, one steep-tension drill and one hook drill. Alternate heel and toe hooks across lower-volume weeks.

| Drill | Dose | Rest | Intensity | What it trains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sloper position mapping | 2 attempts of a 3–5-move low sequence; stop in two supported positions for 2–3 seconds each | 2–3 minutes | RPE 5–6 | Find useful pull direction, palm contact and foot opposition |
| Sloper linking | 2 attempts of a 4–6-move problem; on attempt 2 change one foot or hip position | 2–3 minutes | RPE 6–7 | Maintain contact through weight shifts |
| Feet-stay-on steep climbing | 2 attempts of 4–6 moves on generous holds; intentionally keep feet connected | 2–3 minutes | RPE 6–7 | Body tension, toe pressure and swing control |
| Square versus hip-turn comparison | 2 attempts of the same 3–5 steep moves: one square, one with a comfortable hip turn or flag | 2–3 minutes | RPE 5–6 | Coordinate hips and opposing feet; find the more efficient beta |
| Heel-hook practice | 2 low sequences: one left, one right; each contains 2–3 gentle placements, loading and releases | 2 minutes | RPE 5–6 | Engage hamstring and hip to reduce arm demand |
| Toe-hook practice | 2 low sequences: one left, one right; each contains 2–3 placements, loading and releases | 2 minutes | RPE 5–6 | Oppose rotation and control the hips with the top of the shoe |

Do not turn failed drill repetitions into extra attempts. If a planned sequence exceeds RPE 7, lower the angle, use larger feet or choose another problem. If it still becomes hard, count that attempt against the week's hard-attempt ceiling and reduce A accordingly.

Sloper cues:
- Brush holds and dry your hands. Use as much comfortable palm/finger contact as the hold permits; an open hand still carries substantial load.
- Move your hips until the pull matches the useful face of the hold. Hips lower under the hand often help a top sloper; a side-facing sloper may need lateral opposition.
- Put weight through the feet before moving a hand. Keep wrists in a comfortable range; do not force an extreme bend.
- “Hips in” is an option, not a universal command. Test a small position change rather than squeezing harder automatically.

Overhang and hook cues:
- Keep ribs and pelvis connected; lightly engage abdominals and glutes. Press or pull through the toes instead of letting the legs trail.
- Set the foot or hook, move the hips, then reach. Use a longer arm when efficient while keeping the shoulder controlled; allow the shoulder blade to move.
- For a heel hook, load it gradually by pulling with the hamstring and hip. Avoid wrenching the knee.
- For a toe hook, lift the toes into the hold with the top of the shoe and use the opposite foot for opposition.
- Practise hooks low enough to step off, with an easy release. Avoid positions where a trapped foot could remain hooked during a fall.

Optional board use: replace a C drill with a large-hold sequence on a shallow board setting at RPE 5–7. Do not add board attempts on top of the cap. Record angle and exact holds. Avoid steep settings that make every move a desperate catch or repeated foot cut.

After climbing: accessory package C. Then cooldown and log.

| Version | Time budget, in order |
|---|---|
| 2 hours | Warm-up 30 min → weakness drills 55 → accessories 20 → cooldown 10 → log 5 |
| 4 hours | Warm-up 30 → weakness drills 80 → food/off-wall break 30 → video and movement review 35 → accessories 20 → cooldown 40 → log 5 |

The two-hour version is the default prescription. Four hours is an optional gym visit with more recovery and learning time, not twice the training dose. Leave earlier when useful work is done.

### Supplementary strength and shoulder work

Do these AFTER climbing, not before hard attempts. Leave at least three good repetitions in reserve. Use a light enough grip that accessory exercises do not become finger training.

| Package | Exercise | Dose | Rest | Intensity and purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Push-up-plus, floor or incline | 2 × 8–12 | 60–90 sec | RPE 6–7; pushing strength and serratus control |
| A | Band or side-lying light-dumbbell external rotation | 2 × 12–15 each side | 30–45 sec between sides/sets | RPE 5–6; rotator-cuff capacity |
| A | Supported wrist extension with light dumbbell | 2 × 12–15 each side; controlled lowering | 45 sec | RPE 5–6; wrist-extensor capacity |
| B | Light chest-supported or one-arm dumbbell row | 2 × 8–12 each side | 60 sec | RPE 6; controlled shoulder-blade movement; no heavy grip |
| B | Dead bug | 2 × 6 each side; exhale for 3 sec per rep | 30–45 sec | RPE 6; resist excessive back arch |
| C | Wall slide | 2 × 8–12 | 30–45 sec | RPE 4–5; shoulder elevation with controlled scapular motion |
| C | Side plank | 2 × 20–30 sec each side | 30–45 sec | RPE 6; resist lateral bending and rotation |
| C | Bent-knee hollow hold | 2 × 15–25 sec | 45 sec | RPE 6; trunk tension; stop before the back arches |

Substitute or omit any painful exercise. Do not train through joint pain. Weeks 4, 8 and 12 use one set of each exercise. During other weeks, when both sets reach the top of the range easily at the prescribed RPE for two exposures, add the smallest load increment OR repetitions, never both.

No additional heavy pull-ups, finger curls or grip workouts during this first block. If you already lift heavily, treat that as part of your total workload and remove overlapping pulling work rather than stacking it on.

A small randomized trial in amateur climbers supports a role for structured strength and neuromuscular exercise in reducing shoulder injuries. The exact package here is a practical coaching selection, not a proven injury-proofing formula. [1]

### Cooldown and mobility

For every two-hour session:
- Walk easily for 5 minutes, RPE 1–2.
- Then one round: 90/90 hip switches × 6 per side; ankle rocks × 8 per side; thoracic rotations × 6 per side; gentle forearm stretch × 20 seconds each side. Move slowly and stay at a mild 2–3/10 stretch; no joint or tendon pain. Rest about 10–15 seconds between movements.

In four-hour A, use a 25-minute easy walk/cycle plus 20 minutes of relaxed mobility and rest. In four-hour B and C, use a 25-minute walk/cycle plus 15 minutes of mobility and rest. Repeat the same mobility round up to twice; the remaining time is relaxed recovery, not prolonged forced stretching.

Cooldowns support a comfortable transition out of the session; do not treat them or stretching as protection against excessive loading.

### Hangboarding and heavy finger loading

No separate hangboarding or campus-board work in these 12 weeks. Climbing itself is already a substantial finger-strength stimulus. With only three months of exposure and three weekly sessions available, movement practice and recovery are the priority.

Hangboarding can improve finger strength: one randomized study showed benefits in intermediate/advanced climbers. It does not establish the safety or necessity of adding it for a three-month climber. A survey found an association between regular fingerboarding and finger symptoms in some less-experienced, higher-performing climbers, but cannot prove causation. [2, 3]

This is not a claim that every beginner must avoid every assisted hang, or that a specific grade/month unlocks safe finger training. Reassess after week 12 if consistently recovered, pain-free and still limited by grip after technique coaching. Any future finger work should replace some climbing load, not automatically add to it. BMC guidance also cautions adults against feet-off or dynamic campus work without substantial experience. [4]

### Weekly progression and plateaus

Progress only when ALL are true:
- No finger, elbow or shoulder pain during the session, afterwards or the next morning.
- Normal movement and no lingering focal tenderness before the next session.
- Good feet and shoulder control persist through the final planned attempts.
- Recovery, sleep and motivation are normal for you.

Then choose ONE:
- Follow the next week's increase in attempts at the same difficulty.
- At unchanged attempt volume, try one slightly harder problem, a longer link, or a small angle increase.
- At unchanged difficulty and volume, improve precision, fewer hand adjustments, fewer foot cuts or lower RPE.

If you repeat the same drill cleanly twice at RPE 6 or less, progress one feature: add 1–2 moves, reduce one generous foothold, or use a small angle increase. Do not simultaneously make holds smaller and terrain steeper.

A week or two without a grade increase is normal. After three well-recovered weeks with no progress in moves, links OR technique:
1. Review footage or ask a coach/stronger climber to identify one limiting movement.
2. Change one project style or one C drill, keeping volume unchanged.
3. If performance is also dropping or soreness is accumulating, take an early deload: about half the attempts and one accessory set for a week.

Do not respond to a plateau by adding another hard day or a hangboard session. Judge a new grade across multiple problems and styles rather than one soft send.

### Fatigue and pain rules

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Mild, diffuse muscle soreness; no focal finger/joint pain; movement normal | Keep the easier RPE range and reduce attempts about 25%. Do not progress that week. |
| Muscle soreness changes movement, lasts beyond roughly 72 hours, or keeps returning between sessions | Recovery visit or rest. On return, cut attempts about 50%; repeat the current week. |
| Very poor sleep, unusual fatigue, or warm-up problems feel about 2 RPE harder than normal | No A. If the warm-up becomes normal and no pain is present, use half-volume B at RPE 5 maximum; otherwise stop. |
| New focal finger/pulley, elbow or shoulder pain, even if mild | Stop the provoking activity and end climbing for the day. Do not repeatedly “test” it or tape/painkiller your way through. Arrange assessment if symptoms persist into the next day, recur or limit normal use. |
| Pop/snap with pain, substantial swelling, sudden weakness, inability to grip/move normally, deformity or numbness after injury | Stop and seek prompt urgent medical assessment. These findings need diagnosis. [5] |
| Skin splits, bleeding or painful raw skin that changes your grip | End climbing; choose activities that do not irritate it. |

Cut the climbing portion short if:
- Two consecutive attempts show a clear loss of control, shorter links or more foot cuts despite a full five-minute rest.
- Forearm pump remains above 3/10 after 5–10 minutes of rest.
- You start catching slips with straight, uncontrolled arms or compensating with a forced crimp.
- You reach the weekly attempt cap, develop focal pain, or cannot make a safe landing decision.

If needed, use one 10-minute off-wall reset. If quality does not return, climbing is finished; do not cycle through repeated resets to extend the session. Do not fill unused time with hangboarding or heavy pulling.

Aim for 8–9 hours of sleep when possible, regular meals and enough food to recover. Keep rest-day cardio easy. No weight-loss phase is needed for this plan.

### Benchmarks at baseline and weeks 4, 8 and 12

Testing REPLACES ordinary work and fits the attempt ceilings. Take 48–72 hours without climbing before benchmark A. Use the same warm-up, holds, angle and rest conditions as closely as possible.

| Test | Protocol | Measure | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Controlled performance | Pick two repeatable large-hold V3–V4 reference problems: one familiar style, one moderately steep. Up to 3 tries each; 3–5 min rest; stop at RPE 8. Choose easier references if necessary. | Sends out of 2; attempts to send; best controlled moves; RPE | Replaces 6 A attempts. In week 1, at most 2 additional normal A attempts remain. |
| Footwork and efficiency | Same two easy V1–V2 problems, 2 attempts each; 90–150 sec rest; RPE 4–6 | Noisy placements, foot readjustments, unnecessary hand adjustments and time. Faster counts only with equal/better control. | Uses 4 B attempts |
| Weak-style execution | One sloper sequence and one juggy 15–30 degree overhang sequence, 2 attempts each; 2–3 min rest; RPE 5–7 | Controlled moves, unplanned foot cuts, successful hook placement/release and RPE | Uses 4 C attempts |

In weeks 4, 8 and 12, B has four further easy attempts and C has two. Week 1's remaining B/C budgets can use ordinary drills.

Use a persistent board/spray-wall sequence if available. Record holds, starting position, wall angle and a short video. If the gym resets a reference, establish a new baseline; do not compare unlike problems as though identical. Once a reference is consistently easy, preserve its result and add a harder reference for the NEXT block.

Success at 12 weeks means more repeatable V4–V5 movement, better weak-style control, and a workload you recover from. A V6 may happen, but is not a required outcome or a reason to force tiny holds or add load.
